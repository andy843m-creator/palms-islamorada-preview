History of Islamorada 🐚

🐚

First peoples

Plantation Key once held a large Native American mound (leveled for construction in 1958). State archaeologists dated artifacts from it to about 1,500 years ago, long before the Spanish arrived, when the Keys were home to Tequesta and Calusa peoples. By the mid-1700s they were gone from the Keys, victims of wars, disease and relocation to Cuba.[15]

🧭

Spanish era

The Spanish first sighted the Keys in 1513 and named them Los Mártires (The Martyrs).[1][5] In 1733 a Spanish treasure fleet was wrecked on the reefs off Islamorada in a hurricane, and Indian Key had been a Spanish trading post before the 1700s.[1]

⚓

The wreckers

A wrecking (salvage) industry thrived here from the late 1700s to the 1870s, and Key West became one of the wealthiest cities in the country.[1] Wrecker Jacob Housman moved to Indian Key in 1831 and developed the community there; its buildings were burned in an attack in 1840.[5] Indian Key, an 11-acre island, had been the first seat of Dade County.[1]

🍍

Pineapples and limes

Settlers from the Bahamas arrived in the mid to late 1800s. The Russell family settled on Matecumbe in the 1850s and the Pinder family in the 1870s. The Pinders opened the first pineapple canning factory, and farmers also raised limes, melons and vegetables.[1]

🌺

The name “Islamorada”

In 1907 William Krome, a surveyor for Henry Flagler's railroad, platted a subdivision on Upper Matecumbe Key called the “Townsite of Islamorada.” The railroad depot and the post office (1908) kept the name alive.[2] According to his wife, Krome said it came from the Spanish isla and morada, meaning “home.”[3]

🚂

The railroad and the hurricane

Flagler's railroad (see below) made Upper Matecumbe a railroad town. On September 2, 1935, the Labor Day Hurricane, the most intense hurricane to strike the U.S. on record, wiped structures off the Matecumbe islands in its path. A rescue train reached Islamorada's station just as the surge hit and was swept off the tracks. Hundreds of people died, including many World War I veterans working on the highway project; estimates vary by source.[7][10][4] The Florida Keys Memorial (the Hurricane Monument) at about MM 81.5 to 81.7 honors the victims.[3][19]

🛣️

The Overseas Highway

The first road opened in 1928.[1] In 1931 a section reached Matecumbe Key, with ferries still needed farther south.[4] After the hurricane the railroad was abandoned, and the Overseas Highway opened July 2, 1938, using the old railroad bridges and roadbed.[4][5]

🎣

Sport Fishing Capital of the World

Islamorada's fishing reputation grew in the early 1900s around bonefish, tarpon and light-tackle fly fishing. Zane Grey and other anglers fished from Flagler's Long Key Fishing Camp.[5] Charter fishing here began developing in the late 1930s, and local guides and captains founded the Islamorada Fishing Club in 1950. Famous guides such as Jimmie Albright guided celebrities including Ted Williams.[6]

🏝️

Village of Islands

Voters approved incorporation on November 4, 1997, and the Legislature's charter created “Islamorada, Village of Islands,” effective December 31, 1997.[8][2] (Some sources give January 1, 1998.[2]) Today the village covers four islands (Plantation, Windley, Upper and Lower Matecumbe) over about 18 miles, roughly MM 90 to MM 72.[1]

Henry Flagler & the Overseas Railroad 🚂

Henry Morrison Flagler (born 1830) was a Standard Oil partner who developed Florida's east coast and built the Florida East Coast Railway.[4] His Key West Extension, called the Over-Sea Railroad, changed Islamorada forever.

  • The railroad reached Miami in 1896. Construction of the Overseas Extension began in April 1905.[4]
  • In 1906 a hurricane wrecked construction at Long Key, and doubters called the project “Flagler's Folly.”[4]
  • May 1907: the Central Supply Depot in Islamorada was fully operational.[4]
  • January 22, 1908: Flagler rode the first train to Knight's Key (Marathon), the line's southern end at that time. In fall 1908 he opened the former Long Key workers' camp as the Long Key Fishing Camp, where novelist Zane Grey was a regular.[4][5]
  • January 22, 1912: Flagler, then 82, rode the first train into Key West.[4][9]
  • May 20, 1913: Flagler died in Palm Beach.[4]
  • 1935: the Labor Day Hurricane ruined the line, and the bankrupt railway could not rebuild. In 1938 the Overseas Highway opened over the surviving bridges and railbed.[4]

Want to see it for yourself? Walk the restored Old Seven Mile Bridge to Pigeon Key (see Things to Do), or visit the Florida Keys History & Discovery Center in Islamorada, whose major themes include the 1935 hurricane and Flagler's railway.[14]

History of Bud N' Mary's Marina 🎣

Bud N' Mary's is right next door to The Palms: its address is 79851 Overseas Highway, MM 79.8 oceanside.[11]

  • Around 1937: after the 1935 hurricane, Capt. Everett Fowler bought the natural boat basin there and built a fish camp, motel and marina, according to Marlin magazine.[12]
  • 1944: New Englanders Bud and Mary Stapleton took over, renamed it Bud N' Mary's, and ran it as a hotel and tackle shop. By 1950 they had five guide boats, a motel and a tackle shop.[12][13] (Another source dates their arrival to the mid-1940s, and says Capt. Don Gurgiolo persuaded them to let him build a small dock and run offshore charters.[6])
  • 1978: veteran guide Richard Stanczyk bought the marina.[13]
  • Today: the marina's own website says it was established in 1944 and is home to over 40 fishing captains and guides, and calls itself the oldest and largest fishing fleet in the Florida Keys. That is the marina's own claim.[11]

Timeline of significant Islamorada events 📜

  • About 1,500 years ago: Artifacts from a Native American mound on Plantation Key date back this far (archaeologist estimate).[15]
  • May 15, 1513: Spanish adventurers sight the Keys and name them Los Mártires.[1]
  • 1733: A Spanish treasure fleet is wrecked on the reefs off Islamorada in a hurricane.[1]
  • Late 1700s to 1870s: Wrecking (salvage) industry thrives in the Keys.[1]
  • 1831 to 1840: Jacob Housman builds up the Indian Key community; its buildings are burned in an attack in 1840.[5]
  • 1850s and 1870s: The Russell and Pinder families settle on Matecumbe; pineapples, limes and a canning factory follow.[1]
  • 1873: Alligator Reef Lighthouse is built off Islamorada.[5]
  • April 1905: Flagler's railway begins construction of the Key West Extension.[4]
  • 1907: William Krome platted the “Townsite of Islamorada”; the railroad's Central Supply Depot is working in Islamorada.[2][4]
  • January 22, 1908: First train reaches Knight's Key; the Islamorada post office opens in 1908.[4][2]
  • January 22, 1912: Flagler rides the first train into Key West.[4][9]
  • 1928: The first road opens in the Keys.[1]
  • September 2, 1935: The Labor Day Hurricane devastates the Upper and Middle Keys and ends the railroad.[7][4]
  • July 2, 1938: The Overseas Highway opens from Card Sound to Key West.[4]
  • 1944: Bud N' Mary's Marina is established at MM 79.8.[11][12]
  • 1950: Local guides and captains found the Islamorada Fishing Club.[6]
  • 1976: The Palms of Islamorada building is listed as built (per MLS listing data; confirm).[16][17]
  • November 4 and December 31, 1997: Voters approve incorporation; “Islamorada, Village of Islands” is effective December 31, 1997.[8][2]
  • 2000 to 2006: The History of Diving Museum is incorporated (2000), opens part-time (2005) and full-time (2006).[5]
  • 2009: The Overseas Highway is named an All-American Road.[5]
  • 2013: The Florida Keys History & Discovery Center is established at the Islander Resort.[5][14]
